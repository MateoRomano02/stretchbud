## App safety disclaimer

This app offers general wellness suggestions and is not medical advice. It does not diagnose, treat, or prevent any condition. Move gently and only within a comfortable range: you should feel mild stretch or light effort, never sharp pain. Stop immediately and do not continue if you feel pain, numbness, tingling, dizziness, chest discomfort, shortness of breath or any unusual symptom. If you are pregnant, recovering from injury or surgery, or have a medical condition (heart, blood pressure, balance, eye, spine, joint, or nerve conditions), talk to a doctor or physiotherapist before using these exercises. Skip any exercise that does not suit you. You use the exercises at your own risk.

## General red-flag symptoms

- Chest pain, pressure or tightness, or shortness of breath: stop and seek emergency help.
- Dizziness, vertigo, fainting, double vision, difficulty speaking or swallowing, or sudden severe headache, especially with neck movement: stop and seek urgent medical help.
- Sudden weakness, numbness or tingling in an arm or leg, face droop, or confusion: possible stroke or nerve emergency, seek emergency help.
- Numbness or weakness in both legs, numbness around the groin/saddle area, or new bladder or bowel control problems: emergency (NHS: call 999 / A&E).
- Severe back or neck pain after injury, or pain that develops suddenly or rapidly worsens, or comes with fever or feeling unwell: urgent medical care (NHS: 111 / urgent GP).
- Pain that wakes you at night, is constant, spreads down an arm or leg, or does not ease in several weeks: see a clinician.
- Sudden vision loss, flashes of light, new floaters, or severe eye pain: urgent eye care.
- Unexplained weight loss, a history of cancer, or visible spinal changes alongside pain: see a clinician (NHS back pain page).

## Chin tuck (cervical retraction) (`chin-tuck`)

- Category: neck; body area: neck / deep neck flexors; posture: seated or standing
- Duration/reps: 5 reps x 5 s hold (app-chosen conservative dose; CCOHS 'head glide' is 20 counts x 5-10)
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Start sitting or standing tall, eyes level, ears over shoulders, shoulders relaxed.
  2. Without lifting or dropping the chin, glide the head straight back horizontally, as if making a double chin. Small, comfortable movement only.
  3. Hold the end pose for about 5 s, breathing normally; keep the chin level.
  4. Release slowly (about 2 s) back to neutral. Repeat.
- Key cue: Slide the head back along a horizontal line, like a drawer closing.
- Common mistake: Nodding the chin down to the chest, or pushing so hard the neck strains.
- Evidence level: **clinical practice**. CCOHS (read) lists the head glide; CUH leaflet (read) lists chin retraction. Blanpied 2017 neck pain CPG (read) recommends exercise for people WITH neck pain with mobility deficits but does not specifically recommend chin tuck; no trial shows a desk-break chin tuck prevents pain. Dose is app-chosen.
- Verification (CORRECTED): Removed invented '1-2 cm' travel figure. CCOHS (read) head glide: 'without lifting your chin, glide your head straight back', hold 20 counts, repeat 5-10; CUH leaflet gives no counts. The 5 reps x 5 s dose is app-chosen (conservative), not from a source. Blanpied 2017 (read via mirror) contains no recommendation for chin tuck/retraction and applies to PATIENTS with neck pain, not to prevention. Added stop rule for arm symptoms.

## Gentle neck rotation (slow turn, not circles) (`neck-rotation`)

- Category: neck; body area: neck / cervical rotators; posture: seated or standing
- Duration/reps: 3 reps per side x 5 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Sit tall, shoulders down, eyes forward.
  2. Slowly turn the head to the left over about 3 s, only as far as comfortable, chin level.
  3. Hold 5 s, return to centre over 3 s.
  4. Repeat to the right; 3 per side.
- Key cue: Turn only to comfortable range; shoulders stay still.
- Common mistake: Jerking, forcing the end range, or tilting the head back while turning.
- Evidence level: **clinical practice**. NHS 'neck rotation' (3 per side, 5 s hold). Mayo and CCOHS include similar turns.

- Contraindications and safety: Stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness, vertigo, visual changes, or pins and needles/numbness in the arms or hands. Extra care with vertigo, vertebrobasilar issues or dizziness history.

## Upper trapezius side-tilt stretch (`upper-trap-side-stretch`)

- Category: neck; body area: neck / upper trapezius; posture: seated
- Duration/reps: 2 reps per side x 15-20 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Sit tall; hold the seat edge with the right hand (or press the right shoulder down).
  2. Slowly tilt the left ear toward the left shoulder over about 3 s, nose forward.
  3. Hold 15-20 s with relaxed breathing; feel a stretch on the right side of the neck.
  4. Return slowly; switch sides. Do not pull on the head.
- Key cue: Shoulder stays down; the head moves, not the shoulder.
- Common mistake: Shrugging the shoulder up to meet the ear, or yanking the head with the hand.
- Evidence level: **clinical practice**. NHS 'neck stretch' (5 s holds x3), Mayo 15-30 s holds, CUH side bend. Hold times differ between sources; 15-20 s chosen as a compromise.

## Levator scapulae stretch (`levator-scapulae-stretch`)

- Category: neck; body area: neck / levator scapulae; posture: seated
- Duration/reps: 2 reps per side x 15-20 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Sit tall holding the chair seat edge with the right hand; turn the head about 45 degrees to the left.
  2. Bring the chin diagonally down toward the left armpit/collarbone, nose pointing to the armpit, using only the weight of the head.
  3. Hold 15-20 s; return slowly; switch sides.
  4. Do not pull on the head with the hand.
- Key cue: Look toward the armpit; stretch is at the back/side of the neck near the shoulder blade.
- Common mistake: Pulling hard with the hand; hunching the shoulder.
- Evidence level: **clinical practice**. Described in the CUH patient leaflet (read), which gives no hold times. Hold time is within CCOHS 10-20 s / ACSM 10-30 s static-stretch ranges.
- Verification (CORRECTED): CUH leaflet (read) describes scalene/levator stretches and warns to stop if dizzy; it gives no holds. 15-20 s is within CCOHS 10-20 s / ACSM 10-30 s. Removed the optional hand-on-head step: overpressure on the head is a needless risk for a general-public app and no source requires it. Blanpied citation removed as support (not exercise-specific).

## Shoulder shrug and drop (`shoulder-shrug-release`)

- Category: shoulders-upper-back; body area: shoulders / upper trapezius; posture: seated or standing
- Duration/reps: 3 reps x 3-5 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Sit or stand tall, arms relaxed at sides.
  2. Inhale and raise both shoulders toward the ears over 2 s until mild tension.
  3. Hold 3-5 s.
  4. Exhale and let the shoulders drop heavily, letting arms hang loose. Repeat 3 times.
- Key cue: Let go completely on the drop.
- Common mistake: Holding the breath; lifting so hard it hurts.
- Evidence level: **expert consensus**. CCOHS recommends 3-5 s holds, 2-3 reps for early tension.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - CCOHS: Office Ergonomics - Stretching at the Workstation - https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/office/stretching.html (page read in full)
- Contraindications and safety: Stop if pain. Low risk.

## Backward shoulder rolls (`shoulder-rolls`)

- Category: shoulders-upper-back; body area: shoulders / scapular region; posture: seated or standing
- Duration/reps: 5 slow backward rolls (then optionally 5 forward)
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Sit or stand tall, arms relaxed.
  2. Lift both shoulders up toward the ears.
  3. Roll them back and then down in a large slow circle (about 3 s per roll), squeezing the blades together at the back.
  4. Repeat 5 times, then optionally 5 forward.
- Key cue: Make big, slow, smooth circles.
- Common mistake: Fast tiny circles; pushing the head forward.
- Evidence level: **expert consensus**. CCOHS: 5 backward then 5 forward.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - CCOHS: Office Ergonomics - Stretching at the Workstation - https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/office/stretching.html (page read in full)
- Contraindications and safety: Stop if pain or clicking with pain. Caution after shoulder injury.

## Scapular squeeze (shoulder blade retraction) (`scapular-squeeze`)

- Category: shoulders-upper-back; body area: upper back / rhomboids, mid-trapezius; posture: seated or standing
- Duration/reps: 10 reps x 5 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Sit tall, arms by sides or elbows bent 90 degrees, thumbs pointing outward.
  2. Draw both shoulder blades back and slightly down, as if tucking them into back pockets, over 2 s.
  3. Hold 5 s; chest opens; do not arch the lower back.
  4. Release over 2 s. Repeat 10 times.
- Key cue: Think 'blades toward spine', not 'shoulders up'.
- Common mistake: Shrugging, flaring the ribs and arching the lower back.
- Evidence level: **clinical practice**. Blanpied 2017 recommends scapulothoracic strengthening for patients with acute neck pain with mobility deficits (Grade B); it does not test or recommend this 5 s desk squeeze. CCOHS chest stretch includes the squeeze without a hold. Dose is app-chosen.
- Verification (CORRECTED): Evidence label downgraded from 'guideline-backed' to 'clinical practice'. Blanpied 2017 (read via mirror): Grade B for ACUTE neck pain with mobility deficits: thoracic manipulation + neck ROM + scapulothoracic and upper-extremity strengthening (to enhance adherence); that is a recommendation for patients and not for a 5 s squeeze in healthy people. CCOHS (read) chest stretch includes 'squeeze your shoulder blades together' but gives no hold. 10 x 5 s is app-chosen.

- Contraindications and safety: Stop if pain. Caution after shoulder or rib injury.

## Doorway / wall chest (pec) stretch (`doorway-pec-stretch`)

- Category: shoulders-upper-back; body area: chest / anterior shoulder; posture: standing at doorway or wall corner
- Duration/reps: 2 reps per side x 15-20 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Stand beside a doorframe or wall; raise the right arm with the elbow bent 90 degrees and just below shoulder height, forearm on the frame.
  2. Step the right foot forward slightly and gently turn the torso away to the left.
  3. Hold 15-20 s; feel a mild stretch across the front of the right chest/shoulder; keep ribs down.
  4. Release; switch sides. (Alternative: hands behind head, elbows back, as in CCOHS.)
- Key cue: Elbow at or just below shoulder height; keep the neck relaxed.
- Common mistake: Letting the lower back arch; forcing past mild stretch.
- Evidence level: **clinical practice**. CCOHS (read) has a chest stretch with hands behind head only. The doorway variant is standard physio practice; no authoritative page read.
- Verification (CORRECTED): The doorway variant is not in CCOHS (read; its chest stretch is hands behind head + squeeze) nor in any source read. Mechanically standard. Corrections: hold 15-20 s (CCOHS 10-20 s; ACSM 10-30 s); keep elbow at or BELOW shoulder height (high-abduction 90/90 position loads the front of the shoulder; step 1 said 'shoulder height'); added thoracic outlet/arm numbness caution.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - Mayo Clinic: workplace stretch videos (neck, upper body, seated, standing) and stretching guide - https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/multimedia/neck-stretches/vid-20084697 (pages returned HTTP 403; content known only from search-result summaries (15-30 s holds), not read directly)
  - CCOHS: Office Ergonomics - Stretching at the Workstation - https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/office/stretching.html (page read in full)
- Contraindications and safety: Stop with pain, tingling or numbness down the arm. Skip with shoulder instability, history of shoulder dislocation, recent shoulder injury/surgery, or thoracic outlet syndrome. Do not force; mild stretch only.

## Seated thoracic extension over chair back (`chair-thoracic-extension`)

- Category: shoulders-upper-back; body area: mid back / thoracic spine; posture: seated on chair with a back
- Duration/reps: 5 reps x 3-5 s hold
- Office-friendly: 2/3
- Steps:
  1. Sit tall on a chair with a supportive back; place hands behind the head (elbows wide) or cross arms on the chest.
  2. Take a breath in and gently lean the upper back backward over the top of the chair back, lifting the chest.
  3. Hold 3-5 s, looking forward or slightly up.
  4. Return upright and exhale. Repeat; move the contact point up/down a few cm.
- Key cue: Bend at the mid back, not the lower back or neck.
- Common mistake: Throwing the head back; chair that tips or has wheels without being locked.
- Evidence level: **general guidance (unverified)**. No source read for this self-mobilisation. Blanpied 2017 recommends clinician-delivered thoracic manipulation for patients with neck pain, not this exercise.
- Verification (UNVERIFIED): No source read describes this exercise. Blanpied 2017 (read) recommends thoracic MANIPULATION delivered by a clinician; that is not a self-extension over a chair back, so the catalog's evidence_note was misleading. Fall/tip risk on office chairs is real. Recommend drop from default rotation; if kept, label 'general guidance' and require a fixed, non-wheeled chair with a backrest below shoulder-blade height.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - Blanpied et al. 2017, JOSPT: Neck Pain: Revision 2017 clinical practice guideline - https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/Neck_Pain_Revision_2017%282%29.pdf (PDF returned HTTP 403; recommendations known from search summaries only (exercise, incl. scapulothoracic strengthening and neck/shoulder endurance, recommended for people WITH neck pain))
  - CCOHS: Office Ergonomics - Stretching at the Workstation - https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/office/stretching.html (page read in full)
- Contraindications and safety: Use a stable, non-wheeled chair. Skip with osteoporosis, recent spine fracture/surgery, or spinal stenosis aggravated by extension. Stop with pain or dizziness.

## Wall angels (`wall-angels`)

- Category: shoulders-upper-back; body area: upper back / scapular stabilisers; posture: standing against wall
- Duration/reps: 8 slow reps
- Office-friendly: 2/3
- Steps:
  1. Stand with back, hips and head close to a wall, feet 10-15 cm from the wall, knees soft.
  2. Place arms against the wall with elbows bent 90 degrees ('goalpost').
  3. Slowly slide the arms up the wall to overhead as far as they go without the lower back or ribs lifting off; 3 s up.
  4. Slide back down 3 s. Repeat 8 times.
- Key cue: Keep the lower back and head touching or close to the wall.
- Common mistake: Arching the back or letting wrists/elbows leave the wall to cheat.
- Evidence level: **general guidance (unverified)**. No source read for wall angels. Blanpied 2017 supports scapulothoracic strengthening for patients with neck pain only, not this exercise.
- Verification (UNVERIFIED): No source read lists wall angels; Blanpied 2017 (read) recommends scapulothoracic strengthening for patients but not this exercise. Biomechanics are benign for most people; keep only as 'general guidance' with the shoulder/thoracic-outlet caution, or drop.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - Blanpied et al. 2017, JOSPT: Neck Pain: Revision 2017 clinical practice guideline - https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/Neck_Pain_Revision_2017%282%29.pdf (PDF returned HTTP 403; recommendations known from search summaries only (exercise, incl. scapulothoracic strengthening and neck/shoulder endurance, recommended for people WITH neck pain))
- Contraindications and safety: Stop with shoulder pinching, pain, or tingling in the arms. Caution with shoulder impingement or thoracic outlet syndrome.

## Standing lower-back lean-back (CCOHS 'standing stretch') (`standing-back-extension`)

- Category: lower-back-hips; body area: lower back / lumbar extension; posture: standing
- Duration/reps: 5-8 reps x 2-3 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, knees slightly bent (not locked).
  2. Place both hands on the lower back / hips, fingers pointing down.
  3. Gently lean back a small amount over the hands (about 2 s), only as far as comfortable.
  4. Hold 2-3 s, then return upright. Repeat.
- Key cue: Small, smooth arc; keep the knees straight.
- Common mistake: Leaning back sharply or holding the breath.
- Evidence level: **clinical practice**. CCOHS (read) 'standing stretch'. No trial shows benefit for people without pain; extension can aggravate stenosis/spondylolisthesis (secondary sources, search snippets only). Dose is app-chosen.
- Verification (CORRECTED): CCOHS (read) standing stretch: 'keep your knees SLIGHTLY BENT', hands on lower back, gently push hands forward while leaning back slightly. Catalog said knees straight; corrected. Removed the 'McKenzie-style' label: StatPearls page could not be read (reCAPTCHA) and McKenzie extension is a directional-preference treatment for patients, not a desk stretch. Contraindications (spinal stenosis, spondylolisthesis, leg symptoms) are consistent with secondary sources (search snippets only, not primary). Rep/hold dose is app-chosen.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - CCOHS: Office Ergonomics - Stretching at the Workstation - https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/office/stretching.html (page read in full)
  - StatPearls: McKenzie Back Exercises - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539720 (search summary only; extension may worsen lumbar spinal stenosis symptoms; contraindications include radicular features and instability)
- Contraindications and safety: Do NOT do with lumbar spinal stenosis, spondylolisthesis, or if it provokes leg pain/pins and needles (should not spread down the leg). Stop if pain increases or radiates. Caution in late pregnancy and with osteoporosis. Do not do deep or sustained extension.

## Standing hip flexor stretch (split stance) (`standing-hip-flexor-stretch`)

- Category: lower-back-hips; body area: hip flexors / front of hip; posture: standing, hand on desk or wall
- Duration/reps: 2 reps per side x 20-30 s hold
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Stand holding a desk or wall for balance; step the left foot back about 60-90 cm, both feet pointing forward.
  2. Bend the right knee slightly and tuck the tailbone under (pelvis tilted back).
  3. Gently shift the hips forward until you feel a stretch at the front of the left hip.
  4. Hold 20-30 s, breathing; return; switch sides.
- Key cue: Tuck the tailbone; do not arch the lower back.
- Common mistake: Arching the back instead of tilting the pelvis; knee collapsing inward.
- Evidence level: **general guidance (unverified)**. No authoritative source read. Mechanics and 20-30 s dose consistent with standard practice and ACSM 10-30 s static stretch range.
- Verification (UNVERIFIED): No authoritative page found or read (Mayo 403; NHS/CCOHS do not list it; only commercial/health-blog pages returned, all with the same technique: back leg straight, front knee slightly bent, pelvic tuck, 30 s). Mechanics in the steps are correct and the dose is within ACSM 10-30 s. Keep as 'general guidance' or drop.

## 20-20-20 eye break (`twenty-twenty-twenty`)

- Category: eyes; body area: eyes / ciliary muscle / tear film; posture: seated
- Duration/reps: 20 s, every 20 min of screen time
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Stop typing and lean back slightly, hands off the keyboard.
  2. Choose an object at least 6 m (20 feet) away, for example through a window.
  3. Look at it for 20 s; breathe and blink fully and softly several times.
  4. Return to the screen.
- Key cue: Look far and blink, do not just stare at a distant spot.
- Common mistake: Looking at another near screen (phone) during the break.
- Evidence level: **weak**. Rule is popularly attributed to optometrist Jeffrey Anshel (origin NOT verified in this research). AAO's computer-use page does not mention the rule. Controlled evidence is null: Johnson & Rosenfield 2023 (Optom Vis Sci, PMID 36473088; n=30, crossover, 40-min tablet task, 20-s breaks every 5, 10, 20 or 40 min vs none) found no effect on symptoms (P=.70), reading speed (P=.93) or accuracy (P=.55); the authors say results do not support 20-s breaks as a treatment for digital eye strain. Talens-Estarelles 2022/2023 (Cont Lens Anterior Eye 46(2), PMID 35963776; n=29, single group, no control, no randomization) showed short-lived symptom reduction while software-enforced reminders were active, returning to prior levels 1 week after they stopped; a letter to the editor and a response followed (Oct 2023). The rule is usually attributed to the American Optometric Association (search snippets); do not write that AAO endorses it. Rule is low-risk and plausibly useful mainly because it prompts breaks and blinking. Safe in-app wording: "A common suggestion; one small uncontrolled study found short-term symptom relief, and a controlled trial found no effect."
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - Johnson & Rosenfield 2023, Optom Vis Sci (PMID 36473088): 20-s breaks and digital eye strain, n=30 crossover - null result (abstract read in the verification round)
  - Talens-Estarelles et al. 2022/2023, Cont Lens Anterior Eye: The effects of breaks on digital eye strain, dry eye and binocular vision: testing the 20-20-20 rule - https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effects-of-breaks-on-digital-eye-strain-dry-eye-and-binocular/ (abstract read; n=29, single group before/after, NO control and NO randomization (it is not an RCT); symptoms improved at 2 weeks, gain not kept 1 week after reminders stopped)
  - American Academy of Ophthalmology: Computer use and eye strain - https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/computer-usage (page read in full; it does NOT mention the 20-20-20 rule; it recommends conscious blinking (about 15/min normal vs 5-7/min at computers))
- Contraindications and safety: Safe for nearly everyone. Persistent eye pain, sudden vision change, flashes or floaters: see an eye professional. Break also suits people with eyestrain from non-screen close work.

## Marching in place (`march-in-place`)

- Category: cardio; body area: legs / cardiovascular; posture: standing
- Duration/reps: 60-90 s
- Office-friendly: 2/3
- Steps:
  1. Stand tall, arms bent at the sides.
  2. March in place, lifting each knee to about hip height or comfortable height; opposite arm swings forward.
  3. Keep a steady rhythm, about 100 steps per minute, soft landings.
  4. Slow down gradually for the last 10 s.
- Key cue: Tall posture, quiet footsteps.
- Common mistake: Slouching; stomping loudly.
- Evidence level: **guideline-backed**. WHO 2020: some activity is better than none; reduce sedentary time (no specific threshold). Light activity breaks improve cardiometabolic markers in trials not fetched here.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - Bull et al. 2020, BJSM: WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7719906/ (PMC full text read in the verification round; 150-300 min/wk moderate activity, muscle strengthening on 2+ days/wk, limit sedentary time (no numeric threshold))
  - Cornell (A. Hedge): reminder software, micro-breaks, 3-S method (sit 20, stand 8, stretch 2 min) - https://news.cornell.edu/node/278909 (search summary only)
- Contraindications and safety: Use supportive shoes; stop with chest pain, dizziness, shortness of breath. Caution with balance issues or cardiac conditions. Quiet; mild sweat at most.

## 5-minute walk (`walk-5-min`)

- Category: cardio; body area: whole body / cardiovascular; posture: walking
- Duration/reps: 5 min
- Office-friendly: 3/3
- Steps:
  1. Stand up and leave the desk, taking your phone or nothing.
  2. Walk at a comfortable brisk pace, around the office, outside or up/down a corridor.
  3. Keep shoulders loose, arms swinging, chin level.
  4. Return and sit with a relaxed posture.
- Key cue: Comfortable, brisk pace where you can still talk.
- Common mistake: Staring at a phone while walking.
- Evidence level: **guideline-backed**. WHO 2020 supports reducing sedentary behaviour and 150-300 min/wk of moderate activity; Cornell's 3-S plan advocates regular standing/moving breaks.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - Bull et al. 2020, BJSM: WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7719906/ (PMC full text read in the verification round; 150-300 min/wk moderate activity, muscle strengthening on 2+ days/wk, limit sedentary time (no numeric threshold))
  - Cornell (A. Hedge): reminder software, micro-breaks, 3-S method (sit 20, stand 8, stretch 2 min) - https://news.cornell.edu/node/278909 (search summary only)
- Contraindications and safety: Stop with chest pain, dizziness or breathlessness; people with cardiovascular, balance, or joint conditions should adjust. Choose safe surfaces.

## Stair climbing (`stair-climb`)

- Category: cardio; body area: legs / cardiovascular; posture: stairs
- Duration/reps: 2-5 min (e.g., 2-3 flights up and down)
- Office-friendly: 1/3
- Steps:
  1. Go to a stairwell; hold the handrail.
  2. Climb at a steady pace, placing the whole foot on each step.
  3. Descend carefully, one step at a time, hand on the rail.
  4. Repeat for 2-5 min; slow down gently.
- Key cue: Whole foot on each step; use the rail.
- Common mistake: Rushing or skipping steps; looking at a phone on stairs.
- Evidence level: **guideline-backed**. Counts toward moderate-to-vigorous activity in WHO 2020 (class level); micro-dose benefit not fetched here.
- Sources (accessed 2026-10-13):
  - Bull et al. 2020, BJSM: WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7719906/ (PMC full text read in the verification round; 150-300 min/wk moderate activity, muscle strengthening on 2+ days/wk, limit sedentary time (no numeric threshold))
- Contraindications and safety: Avoid with knee/hip pain, balance problems, vertigo or cardiac conditions; stop with chest pain or dizziness. Falls risk on stairs. Will raise heart rate and may cause sweat (office-friendly 1).

## Deliberately excluded

- **Full neck circles / head rolls**: Often discouraged by clinicians: end-range loaded extension plus rotation with no clear benefit; gentle single-plane movements (rotation, side tilt, chin tuck) cover the benefit. Based on common clinical advice; no authoritative trial fetched.
- **Neck cracking, forced neck pulling, behind-the-head neck stretches**: Risk of strain; not in any source as a self-exercise.
- **Forward folds, toe touches with head below the heart, inversions, downward dog**: Dizziness, orthostatic issues and raised intraocular pressure risk with glaucoma; unsuitable in office clothes.
- **Glute bridges, floor exercises (cobra, child's pose, lying twists)**: Need floor; not office-friendly. Standing/seated alternatives used.
- **Bodyweight deep squats and jumping jacks, burpees, planks**: Sweat/noise/clothing; squat covered by chair sit-to-stand; jumps stress joints.
- **Arm circles**: Low added value over shoulder rolls/wall angels; risk of shoulder strain in office clothes.
- **Bates-style eye exercises (sunning, eye rolling, long eye-gaze drills)**: No evidence; sunning is unsafe. Palming kept only as a rest break with weak evidence.
- **Ballistic or bouncing stretching, held-breath max stretches**: CCOHS: do not bounce or force; stretches should not hurt.
- **Behind-the-back and cross-body maximum-reach stretches with clothing restriction**: Not enough value; Mayo cross-body shoulder stretch could be added later.

## Research gaps and caveats

- Mayo, Blanpied PDF, AAOS PDF and NHS back exercise pages returned 403/404/unreadable; their details come from search summaries and are flagged in each source's 'verification' field.
- Harvard Health, ACSM, Physiopedia sub-pages, and Cornell ergonomics stretch pages were not read directly.
- Origin of 20-20-20 (credited to Dr Jeffrey Anshel; also attributed to the American Optometric Association) was not verified in a fetched source (unverified).
- The 'Talens-Estarelles 2023 RCT' is a 2022 online / 2023 print single-group study (n=29) without a control. The controlled trial is Johnson & Rosenfield 2023 (n=30), which was null.
- No trial was found proving any single desk micro-exercise prevents pain; evidence mostly supports exercise for people with existing pain (Blanpied 2017) and general activity (WHO 2020).
- Hold times differ by source (NHS 5 s, CCOHS 10-20 s, Mayo 15-30 s); the catalog uses 15-30 s for stretches.
